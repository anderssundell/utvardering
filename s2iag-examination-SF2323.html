Assignment 2a: Interviews or Assignment 2b: Observations Group-based written and oral assignment, graded on a Fail/Pass scale (see Canvas for further instructions). Assignment 3a: Typology Analysis or Assignment 3b: D iscourse A nalysis Group-based written and oral assignment, graded on a Fail/Pass scale (see Canvas for further instructions). Assignment 4: Research P roposal Individual written and oral assignment (research proposal), graded on a Fail/Pass/High Pass scale (see Canvas for further instructions). Assignments are uploaded on Canvas (see folders under each module and/or block ). All assignments need to include the name (s) of the student(s), the course code SF2323 and the heading Assignment 1, Assignment 2 a or Assignment 2b , Assignment 3 a or Assignment 3b , and Assignment 4 , respectively. Please make sure that the paper follows the guidelines of the specific assignment and is uploaded in the correct folder! Remember that correct academic writing (good structure and grammar, proper language, correctly entered references, etc.) is a basic requirement that your assignments need to fulfil in order to be considered for grading at all. The compositions of the groups for the group assignments on Research Question and Design will be randomly selected. We do this because we think it is fairer than letting the students themselves choose and we also think that it can be seen as part of the education to learn how to work together with other students in different constellations. The group compositions will be published on Canvas. The students will be able to choose between doing the assignment on Interviews or the assignment on Observations as well as between doing the assignments on Typology Analysis or the assignment on Discourse Analysis. The compositions of the groups for these assignments will be randomly selected after all students have chosen which assignments to work with . The group composition s will be handled on Canvas . Participation is mandatory at all seminars . Those students who fail to participate (e.g. those who do not participate actively as assigned, arrive late, do not stay throughout the whole seminar, or do not show up at all) will need to write another two pages discussing one of the seminar assignments presented by the other groups. These two extra pages are written individually and uploaded on Canvas in the specific folder Late Assignments . Late assignments will not be discussed in a seminar. If your group misses a deadline, or fail a written seminar assignment, you need to upload a new version of the paper on Canvas in the specific folder for Late Assignments . Dates for failed and/or late assignments will be published on canvas. Academic Writing The three hallmarks of good academic writing are clarity, precision, and intellectual honesty. Unlike some other forms of writing, academic texts should be free from ambiguity, and they should offer the reader plainly stated arguments, not rhetorical tricks. This does not mean that they have to be boring, of course, but it does mean that, whenever there is a conflict between being linguistically elegant and clear, the latter should take precedence. For example, in academic writing, it might be preferable to consistently use the same terms for the same concepts instead of using synonyms, even though this would be dull in other types of writing (such as fiction). To organize the text, a good rule of thumb is to make one point per paragraph. Good academic writing is cumulative; it proceeds from, communicates with, and seeks to contribute to previous work on the topic of choice. It is essential that the author (or authors) gives due credit to previous work. There should never be any doubt as to whether segments of text, ideas, claims or results are the author’s own or drawn from other sources. To ensure this, academic writing requires a transparent system of citation. Never ‘copy and paste’ text from other sources without citing those sources and enclosing direct quotes in quotation marks. Copying text without indicating it is a quotation constitutes plagiarism. Use references consistently and accurately. These requirements hold for all academic texts, from short student assignments and course papers to master theses and academic books and journal articles. AI policy AI is a widely used technology designed to generate unique outputs in response to natural language inputs called prompts. Text-generating apps such as ChatGPT (OpenAI), Copilot (Microsoft), and Gemini (Google) draw from large language models containing vast amounts of data curated from the internet and use text prediction algorithms to generate fluent, human-like outputs. On this course, the A -policy is divided into Permitted, Problematic, and Prohibited Use. Permitted: You are allowed to use AI for personalized support, e.g. to ask for instructions to carry out a specific task or use a tool, e.g. how to create a figure/graph, or to provide an example of how an academic paper can be structured. Problematic: If you want language support, please use rule-based AI apps designed to find and correct textual errors. A n even better option is to sign up for an advising session at the Unit of Academic Language (ASK). Prohibited Use: You are not allowed to use AI for text generation or content revision! Learning occurs during the writing process. By reading, thinking, writing, analy z ing and explaining your thoughts and arguments, you deepen your knowledge, memorize the important issues and learn skills. A chatbot will jeopardize your ability to develop your knowledge and skills. It is moreover considered a form of attempt to deceive during examination and will thus be treated as a case of such (see below) . assignments where students apply the different themes discussed . In module 3 , the introductory lecture is followed by scheduled individual and group supervision. Th e structure of the course offers the students the opportunity to gain knowledge about qualitative research methods in general, but also to get hands-on experience of the strength s and weaknesses of different qualitative techniques. Students work individually as well as together in groups , which facilitate peer-learning. Assignments are discussed in teacher-led small group seminars. The course end s with a research plan , in which the students focus on a qualitative research design and methodology of relevance for a research question of their own choice . Module 1: Research questions, research design, and research ethics Research methodology is a mean s to achieve a certain goal ; to answer a specific research question. The choice of technique for data-collection (for example, whether to do interviews, text analysis, or a combination) depends on the question asked. The same goes for choice of design. Moreover, independently of the question in focus, potential ethical concerns must be taken into consideration. Module 1 focuses on the early phases of the research process , with an emphasis on how to pose theoretically and empirically relevant research questions . It also introduces a comparative approach to research, which allows you to trace causal processes, and within the framework of which the theory testing and development is the focus. Module 1 is concluded in the a ssignment Research Question and D esign , which is a group - based written and oral assignment , graded on a Fail/Pass s cale (see Canvas for further instructions) . Module 2 : Data collection, analysis, and interpretation In this second module of the course students will gain theoretical as well as practical knowledge as regards how to work with qualitative data and material. Module 2 is divided into two main blocks . Each of these blocks in turn focuse s on two different methods for data collection, analysis, and interpretation. Block I focuses on interviews and observations, whereas Block II focuses on typology analysis and discourse analysis. Each block follow s the same rhythm with introductory lectures followed by oral and written group assignments. Students are strongly encouraged to attend the lectures given in both blocks. However, when it comes to the oral and written group assignments, students are only required to take part in one of the assignments for each block. That is, in terms of Block I , students are required to take part either in the assignment on interviews or in the assignment on observations , and in terms of Block II, students are required to take part either in the assignment on typolog y analysis or in the assignment on discourse analysis . In other words: students have a choice between observation s and interviews on the one hand, and, on the other hand, typology analysis and discourse analysis. Module 2 sums up in total two assignments: the a ssignment Interviews or the a ssignment O bservations and the a ssignment Typology Analysis or the a ssignment Discourse analysis . These are all group-based written and oral assignment s , graded on a Fail/Pass scale (see Canvas for further instructions). Module 3 : Research proposal Th e third and final module gives the students an opportunity to show their methodological skills and start thinking about, a s well as start writing on , their master’s thes i s. Module 3 sums up in a Research proposal , which is an individual oral as well as written assignment, graded on a Fail/Pass/High Pass scale. The research p lan should be maximum 4000 words , e xclusive of the reference list (see Canvas for further instructions) . Learning outcomes The purpose of the course is to stimulate the students to acquire the knowledge, skills, and judgment necessary in order to be able to critically assess others’ research as well as design a research project of their own. After completing the course, the students are expected to be able to: Knowledge and understanding Demonstrate general methodological knowledge and understanding of the various steps of the research process, namely problem formulation, theory, design, data collection and analysis. Demonstrate in-depth methodological knowledge in the main areas of qualitative method such as comparative case studies, observations, interviews and various forms of text analysis. Demonstrate knowledge of source-critical problems in qualitative research. Demonstrate knowledge of basic theoretical positions in science. Demonstrate knowledge of research ethics aspects that are relevant to qualitative research. Competence and skills Demonstrate an in-depth ability to distinguish the different steps of the research process – i.e. problem formulation, theory, design, data collection and analysis – and how the different steps of the research process relate to each other. Demonstrate in-depth ability to design and plan a qualitative study. Demonstrate in-depth ability to carry out comparative case studies, observations, interviews and various forms of text analysis in a way that is adequate for the social sciences. Demonstrate the ability to apply source criticism in a way relevant to qualitative research. Independently produce text in accordance with good academic standards, including correct citation techniques and reference management. Express themselves orally and in writing in good and comprehensible English. Judgement and approach Identify and assess ethical issues that are relevant to qualitative research. Evaluate how the choice of method and design is affected by problem formulation and theoretical points of departure.
