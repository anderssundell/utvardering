Assessment
Assessment The course is worth 15 higher education credits. There are three forms of assessments on this course, which must be completed in order to receive a pass for the entire course and to fulfil the learning outcomes. These consist of: 1. Active participation in three seminars with written assignments ( 3 x 2 hec) 2. An oral seminar presentation (1 hec) 3. A written research essay of 3, 000 words (8 hec) (1) Active seminar presentation: There are three seminars on this course and participation is mandatory. You are required to actively prepare for and participate in all three seminars, including written assignments. Each seminar is evaluated with the grade High Pass/Pass or Fail. (2) Oral seminar presentation: You will be required to make one oral seminar presentation on this course. In preparation for the seminar, you will be working with around 4-5 students and prepare a collective group presentation based on the specific instructions for each seminar. The seminars are student-led and based on teamwork. The main responsibility for prompting an engaging seminar discussion lies with the presenters. Therefore, you need to plan the group presentation carefully and prepare questions for discussion. You are assigned a group at the start of the course. Based on an evaluation of your performance in the presentation, you will receive a Pass or Fail. (3) Written essay (3,000 words): Towards the end of the course, you will be able to choose to write on a specific research topic. The research essay should focus on a research problem and issue that incorporates at least two of the three themes that this course has focused on, i.e. security, trade, and migration. You should demonstrate in this essay your knowledge of how and why these themes both conceptually and empirically overlap in diverse ways. Writing the research essay offers you the opportunity to explore in-depth a particular issue and research problem that you find particularly interesting. More detailed instructions can be found on Canvas Module. Your paper is an individual piece of work that you are expected to write in your own words. AI- generated text is not allowed (see policy below). All papers will be checked for plagiarism. The 6 essay is assessed using the grades Fail, Pass, or Pass with Distinction. In case of missed seminars or fail grade • If you are absent from a theme seminar but submitted a paper, you need to complete an extra assignment and write a text of 1,000 words that is a critical analysis of two additional academic articles. Please contact the seminar teacher to receive instructions. Submit the extra assignment in the appropriate submission folder on Canvas. • If you are unable to make an oral presentation, you are referred to the re-take examination. Make a recorded video and upload on Canvas before deadline. • If you are unable to submit a paper to a theme seminar on time, you are referred to the re-take examination. • If your theme seminar paper received a fail-grade, you are to submit a new version of the same paper at the re-take examination, addressing comments from the teacher. • If you are unable to submit the research essay, you are referred to the re-take examination. • If your research seminar essay received a fail-grade, you are to submit a new version of the same paper, addressing the comments raised by the teacher. • Check the dates for re-take examinations listed under Assignments. The re-takes are graded by the respective seminar teacher. Ordagrant ur kursguide EU2215, Guide H25 – avsnittet „Assessment”
Grading
Grading scale To obtain the Pass grade the student must fulfil all the learning outcomes. To obtain Pass with Distinction, the work of the student should show a very good ability to connect theoretical analyses to practical outcomes, a good, independent analytical ability and that written assignments fulfil high academic standards in terms of structure and language. A student must receive a Pass for all four assignments, including the oral presentation, to pass the entire course. All seminar papers (including active participation and performance at the seminars) are evaluated with the grades “pass with distinction”, “pass” or “fail”. For the grade ”pass with distinction” on the course as a whole, a student is required to receive a high pass (VG) for the 7 main research essay (8 HEC) as well as at least one of the theme seminar papers, and pass on the rest. Oral presentation (1 HEC) is evaluated with the grade pass or fail. Academic Writing The three hallmarks of good academic writing are clarity, precision, and intellectual honesty. Unlike some other forms of writing, academic texts should be free from ambiguity, and they should offer the reader plainly stated arguments, not rhetorical tricks. This does not mean that they have to be boring, of course, but it does mean that, whenever there is a conflict between being linguistically elegant and clear, the latter should take precedence. For example, in academic writing, it might be preferable to consistently use the same terms for the same concepts instead of using synonyms, even though this would be dull in other types of writing (such as fiction). To organize the text, a good rule of thumb is to make one point per paragraph. Good academic writing is cumulative; it proceeds from, communicates with, and seeks to contribute to previous work on the topic of choice. It is essential that the author (or authors) gives due credit to previous work. There should never be any doubt as to whether segments of text, ideas, claims or results are the author’s own or drawn from other sources. To ensure this, academic writing requires a transparent system of citation. Never ‘copy and paste’ text from other sources without citing those sources and enclosing direct quotes in quotation marks. Copying text without indicating it is a quotation constitutes plagiarism. Use references consistently and accurately. These requirements hold for all academic texts, from short student assignments and course papers to master theses and academic books and journal articles. Research and experience show that the effectiveness of educational activities relies on collective attention. When someone uses a screen for unrelated activities, it distracts others and diminishes the overall learning experience. AI policy: The use of generative AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT) for writing assignments is limited to brainstorming and outlining purposes only. Direct copying of AI-generated text is strictly prohibited, and AI must not be used for drafting or writing any part of the assignment. These assignments are designed to foster your critical thinking, analytical skills, and the ability to express original analysis and ideas. The purpose of this policy is to ensure the work remains your own and that you continuously practice your own skills in academic writing. Plagiarism: Academic writing is based on the principle of clarity and transparency regarding the source of the information and knowledge conveyed in the text. In academic writing, we use others’ ideas, thoughts, arguments, opinions, and conclusions, but we make the origin of these clear by citing and referencing. We rely on, communicate with, build upon, and credit previous research, authors, and sources—but we do NOT copy them! Copying is plagiarism, i.e., cheating. You can read more on academic writing and plagiarism on the Canvas course page. The Department of Political Science uses sophisticated plagiarism detection systems. Teachers are also skilled at detecting texts that are not independently written through critical reading. We are obliged to report suspected cheating to the Disciplinary Board at GU. Cheating can result in a warning or suspension from the program. Read more on plagiarism at GU’s student portal.
