Assessment
assessment is done on a scale from 1 to 10 where scores 8-10 is equal to VG, 5-7 is equivalent to G, and lower than 5 is U. It is worth 3 credits. Second assessment: groups of students’ oral presentation on a research question provided by the teacher. The group presentation is followed by a discussion with the teacher and other students. The seminar is compulsory, and absence is associated with a written make-up assignment. The assessment is done on a scale from 1 to 10 where scores 6-10 is equivalent to G, and lower than 6 is U. The group presentation is worth 3 credits. Third assessment: students are asked to select a research question among those listed for group presentations (students can thus chose to write on the same topic as their own group presented on, or one of the other topics listed) and write a policy brief by applying the research question to one topic from a given selection. Examples of policy briefs are found here on the V-Dem website: https://v-dem.net/pb.html. Students are strongly 3 encouraged to study the writing style, structure, and content of the Case for Democracy-series of policy briefs on the V-Dem website. A good idea is to model one’s own policy brief on these. The policy brief assignment is max 1,000 words exclusive of references, tables, and figures. As make-up for not submitting on time, students need to write about two additional pages (so around 2,000 words in total instead of the max. 1,000 in the original assignment). The assessment is done on a scale from 1 to 10 where scores 8-10 is equal to VG, 5- 7 is equivalent to G, and lower than 5 is U. The policy brief is worth 3 credits. Fourth assessment is an independent student paper for the course. Course papers should address an issue central to the content of the class. The topic will be selected in consultation with one of the teachers around halfway through the course.
