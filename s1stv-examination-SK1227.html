Policy Briefs – format och bedömning
Policy Briefs: page 6 of 10 Course Name March 5, 2026 u 1. You pick one of the suggested questions 2. You answer it while thinking about and drawing on *all* the readings, slides, arguments we have develope. That means lecture, discussion, everything. To draw on all the readings does not mean you have to cite them *all* explicitly - but you need to consider whether or not someone’s argument or way of thinking applies or supports your argument - and if it is relevant, use it. 3. You do not need a bibliography - simply reference the title or the name, I know which one it is. Word limit: 850 words 4 Grading: You will see: A(argument) -. + S(structure) -. + R(readings) -. + Plus means something is done really well. Minus means sth is lacking seriously. Dot means okay. Three pluses = VG. Pass with Distiction Three minuses = UG. Fail All other combinations: G Pass Argument: The argument needs to be clear and easily understandable to get a plus. If I can piece it together but need to work, that’s a dot. If no argument appears present that’s a minus. Structure: A plus means we have Intro and Conclusion paragraphs that clearly state your argument. Clear means also containing as much information as possible. So for example, it is better to say ”I will argue that protest should be encouraged in consolidated dictatorships” than ”I will argue that whether protest is encouraged should depend on the kind of dictatorship” and definitely better than only stating that ”In what follows I will argue when protest should be encouraged”. Note that the latter tells us nothing other than what we know, namely, you are simply stating that you will adhete to the topic of the essay something we take for granted. The essay should further have a few self-contained paragraphs of about 3-5 sentences each which make one specific argument or points that clearly belong together. A dot means that the argument is not stated clearly nonetheless the exposition flows in a manner that allows me to follow. A minus means paragraphs are super long, points appear jumbled together and the essay is nearly impossible to follow. Also on structure - do not include section headings - ”Intro”, ”Main”, ”Conclusion”. Short essays do not benefit from such. The position and content of the paragraphs are enough to establish what they are about. Leave a blank line between paragraphs it helps when reading. Do not cite or draw much on readings in the Intro. Just state clearly your argument. Do not clarify assumptions or offer definitions there - this is for main body. The Intro is for stating your argument. Readings: A plus means that you are drawing on a bunch of readings while focusing on the main points. You are able to say how your argument builds on each reading point that you bring up. . . . cont. Policy Briefs (cont’): page 7 of 10 Course Name March 5, 2026 u. . . A dot says that I see many of the readings referenced but their connection to your points is unclear. A minus indicates that you do not reference core readings or appear to misunderstand the authors’ points. It is much better to organize paragraphs and the whole essay around the ideas and not the readings. The latter should play a strictly supporting role. So, for example, assume you are inspired to argue that protest should be encouraged in closed dictatorships and not in dictatorships with institutions. You start your paragraph with that - ”Encouraging protest is better in dictatorships that do not allow any form of contestation”, then continue ”As Svolik tells us, such dictatorships are especially likely to block the public from participating (and. . . )”. This is preferable to you starting with - ”let me tell you about how smart Svolik is, and his cool theory and then throw in sth about how I could use to argue for encouraging protests”. When you start with Svolik, it feels you are simply summarizing. When you start with your argument, you are showing me that you can think, you can make arguments and you can use others to support and justify your ideas. These others, they are merely helping you. You are the one driving the train. General writing things: Being clear, using short sentences, drawing on examples, personal experiences always makes the story better. Best not to write your name. I like reading blind as much as I can. I can tell that some (small portion of) essays have been done exclusively with Chat GPT or similar. This results in subpar product and does not get you that many points. It will also be of no help on the final exam. It is much more fun to read, come to lecture, and make exciting arguments about the world - than enter the netherworld of AI.
