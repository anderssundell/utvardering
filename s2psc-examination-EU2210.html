Övergripande examination

Examination
Assessment in this course consists of oral presentations, a written exam, and an individual written paper on a given topic. Students will also create a poster and participate in seminar discussions, where they will present their own work and engage with the work of fellow students. Ordagrant ur kursguide EU2210, Guide H25 – avsnittet „Examination”
Requirements (betygsskala och deadlines)
The grading scale comprises High Pass (VG), Pass (G) and Fail (U). In order to obtain Pass you must fulfill all the learning outcomes (see above) implying pass on all assignments. Both oral and written skills are evaluated.

For a High Pass, the work should show a very good ability to connect theoretical analyses to practical outcomes, a good, independent analytical ability and fulfil high academic standards in terms of structure and language. In order to receive a High Pass on the entire course, at least 50 % of the credits need to be graded High Pass.

Hard deadlines on Canvas apply, so be sure to send in papers on time. In case of a missed deadline, the student is referred to the re-take examination. Ordagrant ur kursguide EU2210, Guide H25 – avsnittet „Requirements”
Language, style, and references
A stringent and concise language is requested. Make sure that the reader can tell when it is your arguments and when you present someone else's arguments. Make active use of references (you can choose the Oxford or the Harvard referencing system, but be consistent), we expect you to indicate page number(s) when referencing. When writing the assignments you are expected to use and refer to academic literature, both from the course list and from other sources. Seminars, Exams, and Written Assignments

1. Tutorial Poster Seminar
Please read the detailed instructions under section 2. Poster Seminar and Hand-in below. Based on those guidelines, you are expected to submit a draft proposal ahead of the seminar. There are no formal requirements for this initial draft, its purpose is to allow you to pitch and develop your ideas for discussion.

During the seminar, you will present and discuss your ideas with your peers and contribute constructively to their work as well. This tutorial seminar offers a valuable opportunity to receive feedback in a collaborative environment, through group discussions led by one or two lecturers.

To benefit fully from the seminar, you are expected to arrive well-prepared and actively engage in the discussions with thoughtful comments and questions.

2. Poster seminar and hand-in: „Wow this is interesting”!
Energy and environmental policymaking in Europe entails that many actors try to influence the policy process within the frame of the EU-institutions. Some actors may even try to pursue their specific interest by trying to change the EU-institutions.

The task for this assignment is to launch a thrilling European environmental or energy related problem that you think is pressing for a sustainable development. It is up to you to choose a specific problem where stakes are high, where actors' interests are contested, and which you think is worth analysing social scientifically. You launch your problem through an argumentation substantiated by recent research.

To stir your mind and tease your imagination here are some hypothetical examples for tentatively researchable problems. How does various actors try to influence EU policy with the goal to profit or stop profitability from the fracking method to extract oil or natural gas? How does various actors try to influence EU policy with the goal to profit or stop profitability from the use pesticides like glyphosate in farming? How does various actors try to influence EU policy with the goal to profit or stop profitability from extensive off or on shore wind mill development? How does various actors try to influence EU policy with the goal to promote European corporations' competitiveness with Chinese producers regarding solar energy technology or batteries for electric vehicles?

This is examined through a poster exhibition in the entrance lobby of Sprängkullsgatan 19. You produce a poster that launch a social science research problem, pitch the problem to the other students in the course by orally presenting the poster, and comment on another student's poster. Your poster should consist of four A3 pages printed in colour.

Whereas the substance goal is to train you in bringing on well-grounded arguments for a social science research problem that you find thrilling, the format goal is to train your oral and visual presentation skills. To spark the curiosity and engagement among the poster exhibition attendants, and thus achieve a convincing pitch, your poster should preferably include pictures, graphic illustrations, sub-headings, colours, bulleted points, and the text should be in huge font and only come as single words and short phrases/sentences.

Higher educational credits: 3.5
Scope: A poster made up by four A3 pages printed in colour and pitched in an exhibition.
Poster exhibition: You and the other students will be distributed into four posters pitching groups and walk the exhibition together engaging in one poster pitch at a time.
Preparation: By the start of your posters pitching group's time slot, along with the other students you arrange your poster on portable stands placed in the lobby entrance of Sprängkullsgatan 19. You are also expected to have gone through a fellow student's poster and prepared questions and comments.

3. Tutorial seminar: Critically summarize previous research
In this theme, you are expected to critically engage with existing academic research related to your chosen topic within the field of European environmental or energy policy. The aim is to identify, analyze, and synthesize key scholarly contributions in a way that will inform and strengthen the literature review section of your final paper.

Select at least four peer-reviewed research articles that are directly relevant to your chosen issue. For each article, assess its main contributions, identifying both strengths and limitations. In addition, examine how the selected articles relate to one another, considering their content, methodological approaches, and theoretical frameworks.

Your analysis should emphasize why these particular articles are significant for your topic and how they collectively contribute to your understanding of the research field. Pay particular attention to the ways in which the studies are conceptually and empirically interconnected, and how they help you to refine your own research focus.

This tutorial seminar is designed as an opportunity to receive formative feedback that will help you refine your final paper. You are encouraged to make the most of this by preparing a concise and structured presentation.

Presentation Guidelines: You are recommended to focus on four core articles and present your findings using two PowerPoint slides:
  • Slide 1: A simple list of the selected articles (properly referenced).
  • Slide 2: A synthesis of the most relevant insights, issues, or themes that these articles raise in relation to your topic.

4. Tutorial seminar: Evaluating theoretical perspectives and specifying a research question
The purpose of this assignment is to help you begin formulating a research-worthy problem that is politically, socially, economically, and/or legally significant within the context of European environmental or energy policy. The goal is to explore and reflect on different theoretical perspectives and use them to develop a clear and focused research question.

You are welcome to choose a topic that interests you—this could be one you've worked on earlier in the course or a new one entirely, such as climate change, fisheries, biodiversity, energy supply, or renewable energy. The key is that it should be clearly related to an environmental or energy issue within Europe, and formulated in a way that allows for meaningful analysis.

Try to articulate why your research question is relevant and worth investigating. This involves linking your topic to theoretical ideas and showing how your question connects to broader academic or policy debates. Part of the exercise is to think through how theory helps us understand the issue and why this issue matters.

In your text, aim to explain why your chosen topic fits with your question and offer a reasoned discussion that addresses the question itself. Bring in arguments to support your line of thinking, but also consider opposing views or uncertainties—this will help sharpen your analysis and make your reasoning more robust.

Feel free to draw on both course readings and other academic sources that help you develop your ideas. The main purpose here is to explore, reflect, and prepare for your final paper, so use this as a chance to try things out and get feedback.

Also, keep in mind the general expectations around structure, referencing, and academic style that apply to all written assignments in the course.

Scope: max 1500 words.

6. Exam (skriftlig tentamen)
The course includes a written examination. It is essential that you register for the exam in advance. Please, consult the course schedule for the exact time and location, as the exam will be conducted on site.

The exam will focus on your ability to critically reflect on the key components of your proposed Master's Thesis. You will be asked to respond to questions concerning:
  • The formulation and relevance of your research question(s)
  • The identification of a research gap
  • The relationship between your proposed study and existing literature
  • How your work is positioned within the broader theoretical landscape
  • Your use and understanding of relevant theoretical perspectives
This examination is intended to help consolidate your thinking about your thesis project and assess your ability to articulate and defend its academic foundation.

7. Seminar and hand-in: tentative outline of a Master's Thesis
As the title suggests, this assignment requires you to draft a tentative outline for a Master's Thesis that is directly connected to the themes covered in this course. Specifically, it should address a topic related to European environmental and/or energy issues. You are expected to reflect on what you have learned during the course and use this knowledge as a foundation to formulate an interesting, clear, and academically rigorous research question. Your text should identify and critically summarize relevant scholarly literature, describe your potential contribution to the research field, and propose a structure for your study. This includes outlining an appropriate method of analysis and data collection, along with a justification for your methodological choices. You should also reflect on the potential findings of your study and discuss their significance within the broader academic and policy context.

While methodological considerations are central to the assignment, it is important to remain grounded in the empirical and theoretical dimensions of the environmental or energy issue you are addressing. One of the main objectives of this assignment is to train your ability to specify a well-defined research problem suitable for a (hypothetical) Master's Thesis. In writing your text, be sure to adhere to the skill outcomes, formal requirements, and academic style guidelines outlined below.

You will present your thesis outline during the final seminar using slides. As part of your preparation, you are expected to read all the texts produced by members of your assigned seminar group carefully and be ready to actively participate in the discussions. In addition to general participation, you will be assigned the role of commentator for two of your fellow students' papers. Together with another student, you will lead a critical and constructive discussion on each assigned paper, helping to identify strengths and suggest areas for improvement. To prepare as thoroughly as possible, please review the checklist provided below.

The purpose of the seminar is to develop your ability to argue orally and reflect critically on your ideas in dialogue with others. This collaborative discussion will strengthen your analytical and argumentative skills and give you valuable insights into how others approach similar topics, broadening your understanding of the course themes. Please note that your performance in the seminar, both in presenting your work and engaging with others, will have an impact on your final grade.

Scope: 2500 words.
